Positioning

What this brand is for

Trust Track Travels moves pilgrims between the milestones of Hajj and Umrah, airport to hotel, city to city, mosque to mosque, with the same composure the journey itself calls for. Where the market offers taxi apps and volume operators, we offer one relationship: a chauffeur who knows the route, a price that doesn't move, and a fleet built for guests who notice the difference.

PILLAR 01

Reverence

The service exists to support a sacred journey, not to sell one. Tone, imagery, and vehicle presence should never compete for the guest's attention with what they're there to do.

PILLAR 02

Precision

Fixed pricing, exact timing, licensed compliance. The case against ad hoc taxis and street drivers is operational discipline, stated plainly and delivered every time.

PILLAR 03

Discretion

Understated luxury. The fleet should read as well-kept, not branded. A vehicle is a means to the journey, not a billboard for the company.

PILLAR 04

Continuity

One provider across the whole ground journey, airport, city, Ziyarat, and in time visa and hotel, rather than a different vendor at every leg.

We are
  • Composed, not flashy
  • Warm, not familiar
  • Exacting, not clinical
  • International, not generic
We are not
  • A budget taxi app competing on lowest fare
  • A tourism brand selling excitement
  • A logo-wrapped fleet chasing visibility
  • A vendor the guest has to re-explain their trip to at every leg

The rule that falls out of this: Brass never carries text on Cream or Paper, it fails contrast outright (2.77:1). Reserve it for rules, borders, icons, and large graphic numerals on light backgrounds; on Deep Green it works fine as text (5.30:1) for eyebrows and labels. Sage text on light backgrounds should stay large or bold; at small sizes it sits just under AA, so it's fine for captions but wrong for body copy. One retroactive fix: the tier labels in the package guide currently set Brass on Cream for the "TIER 01" style eyebrows; move those to Deep Green or Ink, or move them onto a Deep Green chip, when that document is next touched.

Voice & Tone

How the brand talks

State what happens, don't sell the feeling. The service is dignified because the delivery is exact, not because the copy says so.

AvoidUse instead
Amazing deals on your dream Umrah trip!!
Fixed fares for every leg of your journey, published upfront.
We're honoured to serve you, valued guest
Your chauffeur will be waiting at arrivals with your name.
Book now before prices go up!
Prices are fixed year-round outside the published peak-season window.
The ultimate luxury pilgrimage experience
A chauffeur who knows the route and a price that doesn't move.
Working rule: if a sentence would read the same on a beach-resort ad, rewrite it. Specificity is the tell that separates this brand from a generic travel operator, name the route, the timing, the vehicle, the policy, rather than the adjective.
Imagery & Motifs

What the brand looks at

✓ Real vehicles, real drivers, real routes, not stock photography of generic travelers
✓ The Haramain Expressway, terminal exteriors, hotel forecourts, the texture of the actual journey
✓ Architecture and skyline over close-ups of worship, respectful distance from devotional moments
✓ The journey line and waypoint motif from the package guide as the recurring structural device
✓ Thin-line iconography in Ink or Brass, never filled or rounded-cartoon style
✓ Golden-hour and early-morning light, matching actual pickup times, not studio lighting
Avoid: stock imagery of anonymous "happy tourists," staged smiling-family clichés, and any commercial-feeling close-up of the Kaaba or the interior of Masjid al-Haram used as a marketing backdrop. Photograph the service, not the sacred site itself.
Vehicle Livery

The fleet is not a billboard

Discretion, Pillar 03, applies most directly here. Guests booking a premium chauffeur service are choosing to be unremarkable on the road, not branded.

Exterior
One discreet decal, rear door or lower trunk edge, wordmark only, Deep Green or brushed-metal finish. No full wraps, no roof signage, no window decals.
Interior
A single seatback card (Cream stock, Ink text, Spectral headline) with WiFi details and the WhatsApp support number. No dashboard clutter, no hanging air fresheners with branding.
Driver
A simple lapel pin or ID badge in Deep Green and Brass, name and language flags, rather than a branded uniform. Professional plainclothes over a company polo shirt.
Welcome pack
Bottled water with a small Brass-on-Cream label, dates on request. The pack is the brand touchpoint the guest actually holds; it earns more design attention than the exterior does.
