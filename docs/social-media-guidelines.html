01 · Positioning

What we are selling

Chauffeur-grade ground transport for pilgrims travelling to Makkah and Madinah from the UK, the US and Pakistan. One provider for the whole ground journey, at a price that is fixed before you fly.

The single claim

The fare is set before you land. Every competitor makes you ask what it will cost. We publish it. Almost every post should ladder back to that in some way — a price, a timing, a policy, a thing that does not change.

Who is watching

Mostly an adult child booking on behalf of parents, on a phone, often at night, weeks before travel. They are anxious about being overcharged at the kerb and about their parents being stranded at arrivals. Post to that person, not to a generic "traveller".

The four pillars, and what each one means for a post
PillarOn social this means
ReverenceThe journey is the subject, not us. No hype, no emoji-stacked captions, no competing with what the guest is there to do.
PrecisionName the route, the fare, the timing, the policy. Specifics are the whole personality.
DiscretionUnderstated. The fleet is unbranded in real life, so it should not look like a logo parade on the feed either.
ContinuityOne provider, one thread, the whole trip. Say so when it is relevant.

03 · Type

Three faces, fixed roles

All three are free on Google Fonts and available in Canva. Mixing a face outside its role is the fastest way to make a post look like it came from a different company.

Display

Makkah to Madinah

Jost Light (300), uppercase. Headlines and the big statement on a post. It comes straight from the wordmark in our logo. Never below 18px on screen.

Body

Fixed fares for every leg of the journey, published upfront.

Archivo (400 / 500 / 600). All running text, captions inside graphics, button labels. Never below 14px.

Numbers

SAR 1,450 · MKH → MED · 4.5–5.5 HR

JetBrains Mono (400 / 500 / 700). Every fare, route code, distance, duration and timestamp. This is the instrument voice — it is what makes a price look like a published figure rather than a claim.

Two habits that carry the brand

Wide-tracked small caps. Labels are set in JetBrains Mono, uppercase, with letter-spacing around 0.16–0.22em. Every eyebrow on the site does this.

The chamfer. Buttons and cards have the top-right corner cut at 45°, about 14px. Never a rounded corner — it comes from the bevel on the pillars in the monogram.

Where to get them

Search Jost, Archivo and JetBrains Mono in Canva's font picker, or download from fonts.google.com. All three are free for commercial use.

If a platform will not let you embed them (some ad tools), fall back to Futura → Century Gothic for display and Inter → Helvetica for body. Never Times, never Papyrus, never a script face.

04 · Logo & profile

The mark on social

Do

  • Use the monogram only for profile pictures — the three-pillar TTT. The full lockup is illegible at 40px.
  • Keep it white on Dusk Indigo. That is the only profile-picture treatment.
  • Give it clear space of at least one cap-height on every side.
  • On post graphics, place the wordmark small in a corner, one size, never centred as a hero.

Don't

  • Stretch, rotate, add a shadow, glow or outline.
  • Recolour it. It is white on dark, or Indigo on Bone. Nothing else.
  • Put it over busy photography without a solid panel behind it.
  • Pair it with a partner or hotel logo at equal size without sign-off.
  • Watermark every photo. Discretion is a brand pillar — one small mark per post is plenty.

What a post looks like

Three layouts cover almost everything. Build them once in Canva as templates and swap the numbers. The structure is always the same: a label, one fact set large, a supporting line, and the WhatsApp CTA.

The hatched bands are the unsafe zones — platform UI sits there. Keep all text and the CTA out of the top and bottom 14%.

Rules that apply to all three

  • One idea per post. One fare, or one tip, or one route — never three.
  • The number is the hero. Set it in JetBrains Mono Bold, bigger than anything else.
  • Exactly one Terracotta element. Usually the CTA.
  • Generous margins. Nothing closer than ~6% to the edge.
  • Sentence case in body text, uppercase only for display and labels.

Carousels

Slide 1 is the hook — a single question or figure. Slides 2–4 answer it. The last slide is always the CTA on Terracotta. Keep to five slides; a fare breakdown that needs eight slides belongs on the website instead, so link there.

Never put the whole tariff on a carousel. Prices change; the site is the single source of truth and the one place we update.

06 · What to post

Six things worth posting

Rotate through these. If a post idea does not fit one of the six, it probably does not belong on this account.

PillarWhy it worksExample
The fixed fare The core claim, stated plainly. Highest-converting format. "Jeddah airport → Makkah. SAR 550, Signature Sedan. That is the price whether you land at 2pm or 2am."
What a leg involves Removes the unknown. People book what they can picture. "Makkah to Madinah is ~450 km and 4.5–5.5 hours. One rest break, water in the cabin, Miqat stop on the way back."
Practical guidance Gets saved and shared. Builds the account between bookings. "How early to leave your hotel for a Madinah departure: 3.5 hours international, 2 hours domestic."
The fleet Answers "will we all fit with the bags?" — the real question. "Executive Van: 7 guests, 7 bags. One vehicle, one fare, no second car."
Ziyarat sites Educational and respectful. Broadest reach. "Masjid Quba, the first mosque built in Islam, and the first stop on the Madinah route."
The service, quietly Proof without boasting. Real, never staged. A name board at arrivals. The welcome pack. The expressway at first light.

A rough mix that works: half practical and route content, a quarter fares, a quarter Ziyarat and service. Three to four feed posts a week is plenty — consistency beats volume, and a quiet, exact account suits this brand far better than a busy one.

07 · Captions & CTA

How to write the caption

The formula

Line 1 — the specific fact. A route, a fare, a timing. No throat-clearing, no "Did you know?"

Line 2–3 — what it means for them. Why that fact removes a worry.

Line 4 — the ask. Always WhatsApp, always the same wording.

Then hashtags, below a line break, 8–12 of them. Never 30.

Worked example

Jeddah airport to your Makkah hotel is SAR 550 in a Signature Sedan. Per vehicle, not per person. Your chauffeur is inside arrivals with your name, tracks the flight, and waits an hour at no charge if you are late through immigration. Send us your flight number on WhatsApp and we will send the fare back fixed. #UmrahTransport #JeddahAirportTransfer #MakkahTransfer #UmrahUK #UmrahFromLondon #HajjTransport #ZiyaratTour #TrustTrackTravels

The CTA, word for word

Use one of these. Do not invent new ones — the button on the website says the same thing, and matching wording is how people learn their way around.

  • Send your flight details on WhatsApp.
  • Get a fixed quote on WhatsApp.
  • Message us with your dates.

Never "DM for price". We publish prices; asking people to DM for one contradicts the entire brand.

Hashtags

Pick 8–12 from these three groups, mixed:

Service: #UmrahTransport #HajjTransport #MakkahToMadinah #JeddahAirportTransfer #ZiyaratTour #UmrahTaxi

Audience: #UmrahUK #UmrahUSA #UmrahPakistan #UmrahFromLondon #HajjPreparation

Brand: #TrustTrackTravels

Keep them at the bottom, after a line break. Never inside the sentence.

We are

  • Composed, not flashy
  • Warm, not familiar
  • Exacting, not clinical
  • International, not generic

We are not

  • A budget taxi app competing on lowest fare
  • A tourism brand selling excitement
  • A logo-wrapped fleet chasing visibility
  • A vendor you re-explain your trip to at every leg

The working test: if a sentence would read the same on a beach-resort ad, rewrite it. Name the route, the timing, the vehicle or the policy — the specifics are what separate us from a generic travel operator.

Emoji: sparingly, and never in a religious context. A 📍 or → in a route line is fine. Praying-hands, sparkles and fire emoji are not.

09 · Photo & video

What we point the camera at

Shoot

  • Real vehicles, real drivers, real routes
  • The Haramain Expressway, terminal exteriors, hotel forecourts
  • Architecture and skyline at a respectful distance
  • The name board at arrivals, the welcome pack, the cabin
  • Golden hour and early morning — the actual pickup times
  • Thin-line icons in Bone or Camel, never filled cartoon style

Do not shoot or source

  • Close-ups of the Kaaba or the interior of Masjid al-Haram as a marketing backdrop
  • People at prayer, or any devotional moment used to sell
  • Stock photos of anonymous "happy tourists"
  • Staged smiling-family clichés
  • Heavy filters, HDR, or a colour grade that fights the palette

Licensing — read this before sourcing

Use footage we own, or stock under a licence that permits commercial use. Pexels, Coverr and Unsplash are free and safe for this. Re-uploading clips or photos found on Pinterest, Instagram or TikTok is a copyright risk on a business account and can cost us the page.

Keep a record of where every asset came from. If you cannot name the source and the licence, do not post it.

Video

Calm and continuous beats fast cuts. Most people watch muted, so anything that matters must be on screen as text. Keep Reels and Shorts under 30 seconds unless there is a real reason. No trending audio that clashes with the subject — a viral sound under Ziyarat footage reads badly.

10 · Never post

The hard lines

These are not style preferences. Each one either misleads a customer, risks the account, or damages the brand in a way that is hard to undo.

  • No invented reviews or testimonials. The previous website carried fabricated guest quotes and they were removed. Post a review only when a real guest gave it, and keep the WhatsApp message or Google review as proof.
  • No price that contradicts the website. The published tariff is the single source of truth. If a post quotes a fare, it must match the site that day.
  • No false urgency. "Only 2 seats left", "prices rise tomorrow", countdown stickers. Our whole claim is that the price does not move.
  • No hidden peak pricing. If a post mentions fares during Hajj or the last ten days of Ramadan, say that the published 25% surcharge applies. Never let someone discover it later.
  • No religious authority. We move people between sites. We do not issue rulings, advise on rituals, or imply a spiritual outcome from booking with us.
  • No naming competitors, and no screenshots of their pricing.
  • No guest faces without written permission, and never children.
  • No engagement bait. "Tag someone who needs this", follow-to-win, comment-for-price. It cheapens a premium service.

During Hajj and the last ten nights of Ramadan, drop the promotional tone entirely. Operational and practical posts only. It is the busiest and most sensitive period of the year, and a sales post lands badly.

12 · Accessibility

Reaching everyone

Every post

Alt text on every image. Describe what is in it, not the file name. "A white GMC Yukon at the Jeddah airport forecourt at sunrise" — not "Trust Track vehicle photo 3".

Burned-in captions on every video. Most people watch muted, and auto-captions mishandle Arabic place names. Check that Miqat, Ziyarat, Dhul Hulaifah and Jannat al-Baqi are spelled correctly.

Readability

Body text never below 14px at export size. Display type never below 18px.

Use the checked colour pairs from section 02. If you are unsure about a combination you invented, do not ship it — pick one from the table.

Do not put essential information in an emoji or in colour alone.

13 · Before you hit post

Pre-post checklist

  • One idea, one fare, one CTA.
  • Any price shown matches the website today.
  • If peak season is mentioned, the 25% surcharge is stated.
  • Only the three brand fonts, in their correct roles.
  • Exactly one Terracotta element.
  • Colour pair is on the checked list in section 02.
  • Text is clear of the top and bottom 14% on 9:16.
  • Alt text written.
  • Video has burned-in captions, place names spelled correctly.
  • No invented review, no urgency, no engagement bait.
  • Every asset's source and licence is recorded.
  • CTA points to WhatsApp, worded as in section 07.

Questions on anything here, or something that does not fit a template — ask before publishing rather than after. A post is easy to delete; a screenshot of it is not.
